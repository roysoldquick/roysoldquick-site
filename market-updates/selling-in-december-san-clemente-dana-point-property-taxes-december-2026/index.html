Selling in December? Property Taxes, the Dec. 10 Deadline and Prop 19 for San Clemente and Dana Point Owners (December 2026)

If you're selling, or thinking about it, in San Clemente, Dana Point or Capistrano Beach, December is when property taxes quietly shape your net. Here's how the Dec. 10 deadline, escrow proration and Prop 19 work, with the math in real dollars.

By Roy Quick, Realtor® · LUXRE Realty
November 30, 2026 · 6 min read · 980-416-6412

The short version

  • Dec. 10 deadline: Orange County's first property tax installment is late after December 10, with a 10% penalty (OC Treasurer-Tax Collector).
  • Selling this winter? Taxes are prorated in escrow. On a $12,000 yearly bill, closing December 18 versus January 15 shifts about $920 between you and the buyer (my own math).
  • 55 or older? Prop 19 can let you carry your low tax base to your next California home. On my example below, that's about $8,000 a year in savings.
  • The market: Orange County listings are up about 10% from a year ago and pending sales are down about 23% (Realtor.com via FRED). Rates eased to 7.21% (Freddie Mac).

The dates every Orange County owner should know

Here's the calendar from the Orange County Treasurer-Tax Collector and the Assessor. The tax year runs July 1 to June 30.

Two of those matter right now. If you've moved out and rented your home, or it's no longer your main residence, December 10 is the date to end the homeowners' exemption without a penalty. And if your home was affected by this fall's coastal flooding or fires, the Treasurer-Tax Collector put out a notice on October 21 about possible relief on property tax payments. It's worth a call before you pay.

How property taxes work when you sell in San Clemente

Secured property taxes are prorated by how long you owned the home. You pay for your days in the tax year, the buyer pays for theirs, and escrow settles up. The first installment covers July through December, so the closing date decides who owes whom.

Let's say your annual tax bill is $12,000, so each installment is $6,000:

Closing dateYour share of the yearYou already paidEscrow result
December 18$5,622 (171 days)$6,000Buyer credits you about $378
January 15$6,542 (199 days)$6,000You credit the buyer about $542

My own math on a 365-day tax year. Your escrow officer's exact day count can differ by a day or so.

That's about a $920 swing between a mid-December and mid-January close. It's not a reason to pick a closing date by itself, but it belongs in your net sheet. The bigger risk is the deadline itself: if you're in escrow and assume the title company will pay the first installment, double-check. Miss December 10 on a $6,000 installment and the 10% penalty is $600, and that's on you as the owner of record.

Prop 19: keeping your low tax base when you move

This is the one I wish more long-time San Clemente and Dana Point owners knew about. If you're 55 or older (or severely disabled, or lost a home to a disaster), Prop 19 lets you transfer your home's taxable value to a replacement home anywhere in California, up to three times, as long as you buy within two years of selling. If the new home costs more, the difference is added on top of your old value.

Here's what that can look like. Say you bought in Talega years ago and your taxable value is $700,000. You sell at San Clemente's 12-month median of $1,797,000 (MLS data via RealaTrends, as of October 30).

  • Downsize to a $1,500,000 home (right at Orange County's October median, per C.A.R.): your $700,000 value carries over. At the 1% base rate, that's about $7,000 a year instead of about $15,000. Savings: about $8,000 a year.
  • Move up to a $2,000,000 home in Monarch Beach: your new taxable value is $700,000 plus the $203,000 difference, or $903,000. That's about $9,030 a year instead of $20,000. Savings: about $10,970 a year.

My own math, 1% base rate only. Voter-approved bonds and special assessments add to every bill, and the Assessor applies the exact rules, so file your claim and confirm with them.

Over ten years, that's roughly $80,000 to $110,000 staying in your pocket (simple math, before the small yearly increases on both bills). For a lot of empty-nesters I talk to, that changes the whole "should we move?" conversation.

Orange County: more homes, fewer buyers

Here's the bigger picture. Orange County had 4,692 active listings in October 2026, up from 4,252 a year earlier (about 10% more), while pending sales fell to 1,466 from 1,902 (about 23% fewer), according to Realtor.com data published by the St. Louis Fed. Put another way, about 31 homes were in escrow for every 100 on the market, down from about 45 a year ago (my own math).

Prices are holding up: C.A.R. reported an Orange County median of $1,500,000 in October, but sales were down 20.7% from a year ago. And rates finally gave a little back. Freddie Mac's 30-year average dipped to 7.21% from 7.28%. On a San Clemente median-priced home with 20% down, that's about $68 a month less for a buyer: $9,768 versus $9,836, principal and interest (my own math).

For sellers in San Clemente, Dana Point and Capistrano Beach, the takeaway is the same as it's been all fall: buyers have options, so price, condition and terms do the work. The good news is that holiday inventory usually thins out, so a well-prepared December listing has less to compete with.

Questions I hear this week

When are Orange County property taxes due in December?

The first installment of secured property taxes is due November 1 and becomes delinquent after December 10. If it isn't postmarked, received or paid online by midnight on December 10, the Orange County Treasurer-Tax Collector adds a 10% penalty. The second installment is due February 1 and is late after April 10, with a 10% penalty plus a $23 cost. If a deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.

Who pays property taxes when you sell a house in Orange County in December?

Secured property taxes are prorated by time of ownership, so the seller pays for the days they owned the home in the July 1 to June 30 tax year and the buyer pays for the rest. Escrow settles the difference. For example, on a $12,000 annual bill, a seller closing on December 18 owes about $5,622 for July 1 through December 18. If they already paid the $6,000 first installment, the buyer credits them about $378 (my own math; your escrow officer's exact days may differ).

Can I keep my low property tax if I sell my San Clemente home and buy another one?

If you're 55 or older (or qualify as severely disabled, or lost a home to a disaster), Prop 19 lets you transfer your home's taxable value to a replacement home anywhere in California, up to three times in your lifetime, if you buy within two years of selling. If the new home costs more than the one you sold, the difference is added to your transferred value. The county assessor makes the final call, so file the claim and ask them about your situation.

Is December a good time to sell a house in San Clemente or Dana Point?

It can be, if the home is priced right. Orange County had 4,692 active listings in October 2026, about 10% more than a year earlier, while pending sales fell about 23% to 1,466 (Realtor.com data via FRED). Inventory usually thins out over the holidays, so a well-priced December listing faces fewer competitors, but buyers have choices and are negotiating.

What are mortgage rates right now?

Freddie Mac's survey for November 25, 2026 put the average 30-year fixed at 7.21%, down from 7.28% the week before. The 15-year fixed was 6.54%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.23%. It's a national average, and individual quotes vary by lender and borrower.
