Sell or Lease Your San Clemente Home? November 2026 Market Update

Rates eased a little this week, rents are high, and buyers are negotiating harder at the top of the market. If you own in San Clemente, Dana Point or Capistrano Beach, here's how I'd think about selling versus leasing right now.

By Roy Quick, Realtor® · LUXRE Realty
November 2, 2026 · 5 min read · 980-416-6412

The short version

  • Rates: the 30-year fixed dipped to 7.24% this week, from 7.32% (Freddie Mac).
  • Rents: San Clemente 3-bedroom rentals are listed at about $6,500 a month on average (Zillow).
  • Orange County: homes priced above $2.5M closed an average of 4.6% below asking, while homes under $2.5M closed just 1.4% below.
  • Local news: repairs continue on Capistrano Beach's Beach Road, and the Norte Fire reached 93% containment.

Hi neighbors, Roy here with this week's Market Update. Last week I covered prices across San Clemente, Dana Point and Orange County. This week I want to answer the question I get asked more than any other right now: "Roy, should I sell my house, or should I lease it out?"

There's no one-size answer, but there is a way to run the numbers. Let's do it together.

Rates eased a little this week

Freddie Mac's survey for October 29 put the average 30-year fixed at 7.24%, down from 7.32% the week before. The 15-year fixed slipped to 6.60%. That's still well above a year ago, when the 30-year was 6.17%.

Small moves matter at San Clemente prices. On a $2.225 million home (roughly the September single-family median here) with 20% down, the principal and interest on a $1.78 million loan is about $12,131 a month at 7.24%, about $96 less than last week. It's not a game changer, but it's a step in the right direction for buyers. (My own math, principal and interest only.)

Here's the other side of the equation. According to Zillow's October 13 update, there were 181 rentals available in San Clemente, and the average asking rent across all of them was about $5,747 a month. By size:

Sell or lease? Let's run the numbers

Let's say you own a 3-bedroom single-family home in San Clemente worth around $2.2 million, and you locked in a 3% mortgage a few years ago with about $800,000 left on it.

  • Your payment: principal and interest on $800,000 at 3% is about $3,373 a month.
  • Your rent: at the 3-bedroom average of about $6,500, that leaves roughly $3,100 a month before property taxes, insurance, maintenance, vacancy and management.
  • Your rate is an asset: the same $800,000 borrowed at today's 7.24% would cost about $5,452 a month. That's over $2,000 a month you keep by holding onto your loan.
  • The return: $6,500 a month is $78,000 a year, about a 3.5% gross return on a $2.2 million home before expenses.

My own math, for illustration only. Your taxes, HOA, insurance and the home's actual rent will change the picture. This isn't tax or legal advice, so talk with your CPA before deciding.

So what does that tell us? Leasing tends to make the most sense when you have a low-rate loan, the rent covers your costs with room to spare, and you're comfortable being a landlord (or hiring a good property manager). Selling tends to make the most sense when you need the equity for your next move, a 3.5% gross return doesn't excite you, or your home sits in the price range where buyers have the most leverage. More on that next.

Orange County: where buyers are negotiating

The weekly Orange County Housing Report for October 26 showed 5,054 homes for sale countywide and 1,522 in escrow, down from 1,573 the week before. Demand is still soft for this time of year.

The most useful detail for sellers is how deals are closing. Homes priced above $2.5 million sold for an average of 4.6% below their asking price, wider than 3.1% the week before. Homes under $2.5 million closed only 1.4% below asking. Homes between $1 million and $2 million had a median of just 41 days on market, one of the most active ranges in the county.

If you own a higher-end South OC home, that 4.6% gap matters. On a $3 million home, it's about $138,000. That's why I put so much work into pricing and presentation before a home ever hits the market.

Capistrano Beach: Beach Road update

Waves and storm surge from Tropical Storm Rachel added to the damage on Beach Road in Capistrano Beach, which Hurricane Marie's swell first hit over Labor Day weekend. As of October 12, the Dana Point Times reported 7 homes uninhabitable, 2 demolished, 1 scheduled for demolition and 13 with moderate damage in the private Capistrano Bay District.

Contractors have been placing boulders along the shoreline with Coastal Commission approval, and crews began placing up to 9,000 cubic yards of sand at Capistrano Beach on October 14, a project estimated at $520,000. If you own along the coast, now is a good time to review your insurance and any coastal permits before you need them.

Norte Fire: 93% contained

The Norte Fire held at 570 acres and reached 93% containment by October 21, according to the Dana Point Times. The seven damaged or destroyed homes were all in San Juan Capistrano, and the cause is still under investigation. With rain in the forecast this season, officials have warned that neighborhoods near the burn scar could see more mud and debris flow risk, so keep an eye on city alerts if you live nearby.

My take this week

  • Got a low-rate mortgage? Don't sell on autopilot. Run the lease numbers first. Your rate may be worth more than you think.
  • Above $2.5 million? Buyers have room to negotiate. Price it right from day one and make it shine.
  • Between $1 million and $2 million? This is one of the busiest ranges in the county. If your home is in good shape, it can move.

Questions I hear this week

How much does a house rent for in San Clemente?

As of Zillow's October 13, 2026 update, the average asking rent across all San Clemente rentals was about $5,747 a month. By size, 2-bedrooms averaged about $4,200, 3-bedrooms about $6,500 and 4-bedrooms about $11,498. These are asking rents on listed rentals, so your actual rent depends on the home, the view and the condition.

Should I sell or rent out my house in San Clemente?

If you have a low-rate mortgage and the rent comfortably covers your payment, taxes, insurance and upkeep, leasing can make sense and lets you keep your rate. If you need the equity, don't want to be a landlord, or your home is priced above about $2.5 million where buyers are negotiating harder, selling sooner may be the better move.
