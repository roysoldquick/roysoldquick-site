Rates Jumped Back to 7.28%: What San Clemente and Dana Point Sellers Can Do Now (November 2026)

After four straight weeks of relief, mortgage rates jumped back up this week. If you're selling in San Clemente, Dana Point or Capistrano Beach this winter, here's what that does to your buyer's payment, and why a seller credit can beat a price cut, with the math in real dollars.

By Roy Quick, Realtor® · LUXRE Realty
November 23, 2026 · 5 min read · 980-416-6412

The short version

  • Rates jumped: the 30-year fixed rose to 7.28% from 7.11%, ending a four-week slide (Freddie Mac, Nov. 19).
  • What it costs: on a San Clemente median-priced home with 20% down, that's about $165 a month more in one week (my own math).
  • Prices held, sales didn't: Orange County's October median hit $1,500,000, but sales fell 20.7% from a year ago (C.A.R.).
  • My take: a seller credit toward a rate buydown can do about 10 times more for a buyer's first-year payment than the same dollars off the price.

Hi neighbors, Roy here, and happy Thanksgiving week. Last week I wrote about El Niño and what a wet winter means for your home. This week the news is about money, not weather: after four straight weeks of falling rates, mortgage rates jumped back up. If you're thinking about selling this winter, here's what that does to your buyers, and what you can do about it.

Rates are back above 7.25%

Freddie Mac's survey for November 19 put the 30-year fixed at 7.28%, up from 7.11% a week earlier. The 15-year rose to 6.66% from 6.45%. A year ago, the 30-year was 6.26%.

Why the jump? October's inflation report came in hotter than expected, which has markets bracing for a possible Federal Reserve rate hike at its December 8-9 meeting, and the 10-year Treasury yield pushed back toward 5% this week (Mortgage Professional America). Freddie Mac's chief economist, Sam Khater, made a point I agree with: for buyers, comparing multiple rate quotes "can potentially save them thousands" over the life of a loan.

What 7.28% means on a San Clemente home

As I shared last week, San Clemente's 12-month median sale price was $1,797,000 as of October 30, and homes went under contract in a median of 14 days (MLS data via RealaTrends). Here's what a buyer pays on that home with 20% down, a $1,437,600 loan, principal and interest only:

So in one week, the same house got about $165 a month more expensive for a buyer, and it's about $975 a month more than a year ago. That's the gap buyers are feeling, and it's why the ones still shopping are negotiating.

Orange County: prices up, buyers pulling back

The California Association of Realtors' October report shows Orange County's median sale price at $1,500,000, up from $1,391,000 a year earlier (about 7.8%, my own math) and up 1.4% from September. But sales fell 20.7% from October 2025 and 15.3% from September (Korea Daily, citing C.A.R.). Statewide, sales dropped to their lowest level in nearly three years.

C.A.R.'s chief economist, Jordan Levine, said "high inflation and a cooling stock market are creating renewed headwinds for the housing market." In plain English: homes that sell are still selling for strong prices, but there are fewer buyers to go around.

Dana Point and Capistrano Beach: strong prices, more choices for buyers

Dana Point's median close price was $2.02 million in the third quarter, about 12% higher than the same quarter of 2025, with 102 closed sales and an average of 70 days on market (Pacific Sotheby's International Realty, closed sales through September 30). As of November 20, Dana Point had 132 active listings (Movoto).

That's healthy pricing. But with rates back above 7.25%, a Dana Point or Capistrano Beach buyer on a $2 million home is looking at a five-figure monthly payment, and they have more than a hundred homes to compare yours to. The homes that sell this winter will be the ones that make the payment work.

Price cut or rate buydown? Let's run the numbers

When buyers push back, most sellers' first instinct is to drop the price. Sometimes that's right. But look at what the same money does in two different places, using the San Clemente median again:

  • Option A, a $34,000 price cut: the buyer's loan drops to $1,410,400, and their payment at 7.28% falls to about $9,650. That saves them about $186 a month.
  • Option B, about $34,000 as a seller credit toward a 2-1 buydown: a 2-1 buydown lowers the buyer's rate by 2 percentage points in year one and 1 percentage point in year two. The buyer pays about $7,965 a month in year one ($1,871 less) and $8,880 in year two ($957 less). Covering those two years of savings costs about $33,932.

My own math, principal and interest only, on a $1,797,000 home with 20% down at 7.28%.

Same dollars from you, about 10 times the first-year payment relief for the buyer. And your sale price stays at $1,797,000, which matters for the next comps on your street.

A few honest caveats. A temporary buydown isn't a lower rate forever; in year three the buyer is back at the full rate unless they refinance. Lenders price buydowns differently, and loan programs cap how much a seller can contribute, so the buyer's lender has to sign off. And a cash buyer doesn't care about rates at all. That's why I like offering a credit "toward the buyer's rate or closing costs" in the listing, so each buyer can use it the way that helps them most.

Around San Clemente and Dana Point this week

  • PCH rockslide in Dana Point: on November 13, a minor rockslide closed Pacific Coast Highway in both directions from Camino Capistrano to Palisades, right through Capistrano Beach, while crews cleared debris (Patch). If you live near the bluffs, it's a good week to look at your slope and drainage before the next storm.
  • High tides and storms: Orange County beach cities were bracing for possible flooding after higher-than-usual tides the weekend of November 15, with thunderstorms in the forecast (Voice of OC).
  • Measure M is done: nearly two-thirds of San Clemente voters opposed the 1-cent sales tax for sand and wildfire prevention, which would have raised about $15 million a year (DredgeWire). The OCTA rail-protection sand is still coming, but there's no long-term city sand plan yet.

My take this week

  • Don't chase the weekly rate. Rates moved $165 a month in one week. You can't control that, but you can control your price and terms.
  • Think credit before cut. For a financed buyer, a seller credit toward a buydown often goes much further than the same dollars off the price.
  • The holidays aren't dead. Fewer homes compete for serious buyers in December. A well-priced home with the right terms can stand out.

Questions I hear this week

What are mortgage rates right now in Orange County?

Freddie Mac's national survey for November 19, 2026 put the average 30-year fixed at 7.28%, up from 7.11% the week before and the first increase after four weekly declines. The 15-year fixed was 6.66%, up from 6.45%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.26%. Freddie Mac's survey is a national average, and individual quotes vary by lender and borrower, so it pays to compare several quotes.

Should I lower my price or offer a rate buydown when selling my home in San Clemente?

It depends on the buyer, but the math often favors a credit. On a $1,797,000 home with 20% down, cutting the price by $34,000 lowers the buyer's payment by about $186 a month at 7.28%. Spending about the same amount on a 2-1 temporary buydown lowers their payment by about $1,871 a month in year one and $957 in year two (my own math, principal and interest only). Lenders price buydowns differently and loan programs cap seller contributions, so the buyer's lender has to confirm the numbers.

Are Orange County home prices still going up?

Yes, as of October. The California Association of Realtors reported an Orange County median sale price of $1,500,000 in October 2026, up from $1,391,000 in October 2025, about 7.8% by my math, and up 1.4% from September. But sales fell 20.7% from a year earlier, so fewer buyers are paying those prices.

Is it a bad idea to list my house during the holidays in Dana Point or San Clemente?

Not necessarily. Fewer homes compete for the buyers who are still out, and holiday buyers tend to be serious. Dana Point had 132 active listings on November 20, 2026 (Movoto). The key this year is pricing and terms: with rates back above 7%, a home priced right with a smart seller credit stands out from the homes that are just waiting for spring.

How much does a 1% change in mortgage rates cost a buyer in San Clemente?

On San Clemente's 12-month median sale price of $1,797,000 with 20% down, principal and interest is about $9,836 a month at 7.28% and about $8,880 at 6.28%, a difference of about $957 a month (my own math). That's the gap a lot of buyers are trying to close right now.
