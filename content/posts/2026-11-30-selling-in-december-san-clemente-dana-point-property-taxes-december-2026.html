Here's the calendar from the Orange County Treasurer-Tax Collector and the Assessor. The tax year runs July 1 to June 30.

Two of those matter right now. If you've moved out and rented your home, or it's no longer your main residence, December 10 is the date to end the homeowners' exemption without a penalty. And if your home was affected by this fall's coastal flooding or fires, the Treasurer-Tax Collector put out a notice on October 21 about possible relief on property tax payments. How property taxes work when you sell in San Clemente

Secured property taxes are prorated by how long you owned the home. You pay for your days in the tax year, the buyer pays for theirs, and escrow settles up. The first installment covers July through December, so the closing date decides who owes whom.

Let's say your annual tax bill is $12,000, so each installment is $6,000:

Closing dateYour share of the yearYou already paidEscrow result
December 18$5,622 (171 days)$6,000Buyer credits you about $378
January 15$6,542 (199 days)$6,000You credit the buyer about $542

My own math on a 365-day tax year. Your escrow officer's exact day count can differ by a day or so.

That's about a $920 swing between a mid-December and mid-January close. It's not a reason to pick a closing date by itself, but it belongs in your net sheet. The bigger risk is the deadline itself: if you're in escrow and assume the title company will pay the first installment, double-check. Miss December 10 on a $6,000 installment and the 10% penalty is $600, and that's on you as the owner of record.

Prop 19: keeping your low tax base when you move

This is the one I wish more long-time San Clemente and Dana Point owners knew about. If you're 55 or older (or severely disabled, or lost a home to a disaster), Prop 19 lets you transfer your home's taxable value to a replacement home anywhere in California, up to three times, as long as you buy within two years of selling. If the new home costs more, the difference is added on top of your old value.

Here's what that can look like. Say you bought in Talega years ago and your taxable value is $700,000. You sell at San Clemente's 12-month median of $1,797,000 (MLS data via RealaTrends, as of October 30).

  • Downsize to a $1,500,000 home (right at Orange County's October median, per C.A.R.): your $700,000 value carries over. At the 1% base rate, that's about $7,000 a year instead of about $15,000. Savings: about $8,000 a year.
  • Move up to a $2,000,000 home in Monarch Beach: your new taxable value is $700,000 plus the $203,000 difference, or $903,000. That's about $9,030 a year instead of $20,000. Savings: about $10,970 a year.

My own math, 1% base rate only. Voter-approved bonds and special assessments add to every bill, and the Assessor applies the exact rules, so file your claim and confirm with them.

Over ten years, that's roughly $80,000 to $110,000 staying in your pocket (simple math, before the small yearly increases on both bills). For a lot of empty-nesters I talk to, that changes the whole "should we move?" conversation.

Orange County: more homes, fewer buyers

Here's the bigger picture. Orange County had 4,692 active listings in October 2026, up from 4,252 a year earlier (about 10% more), while pending sales fell to 1,466 from 1,902 (about 23% fewer), according to Realtor.com data published by the St. Louis Fed. Put another way, about 31 homes were in escrow for every 100 on the market, down from about 45 a year ago (my own math).

Prices are holding up: C.A.R. reported an Orange County median of $1,500,000 in October, but sales were down 20.7% from a year ago. And rates finally gave a little back. Freddie Mac's 30-year average dipped to 7.21% from 7.28%. On a San Clemente median-priced home with 20% down, that's about $68 a month less for a buyer: $9,768 versus $9,836, principal and interest (my own math).

For sellers in San Clemente, Dana Point and Capistrano Beach, the takeaway is the same as it's been all fall: buyers have options, so price, condition and terms do the work. The good news is that holiday inventory usually thins out, so a well-prepared December listing has less to compete with.
