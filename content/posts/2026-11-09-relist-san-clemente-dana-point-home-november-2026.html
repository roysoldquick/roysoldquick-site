Hi neighbors, Roy here with this week's Market Update. The last two weeks I covered prices and the sell-versus-lease math. This week I want to talk to a group of homeowners nobody writes for: the folks whose home was on the market this fall and didn't sell.

If that's you, you're far from alone. A home that didn't sell the first time can absolutely sell the second time, if you change the right things.

Orange County: a wave of listings just came off the market

The weekly Orange County Housing Report for November 2 counted 443 listings delisted in a single week, through cancellations and expirations. A typical week sees about 200. That's my own math at about 2.2 times normal. The report called it a normal pre-holiday move, not a warning sign, and it's a big reason countywide inventory slipped back under 5,000 to 4,945 homes.

Here's what else the report showed:

  • Inventory is still up: 4,945 homes is about 17% more than the 4,209 on the market the same week last year (my own math on the report's figures).
  • Buyers are still closing: 488 homes closed in the prior seven days, up from 326 the week before, one of the strongest weekly totals in a while.
  • Pending sales drifted lower: 1,494 homes were in escrow, down from 1,522.

Countywide, prices are holding up. C.A.R. put the Orange County median at $1,480,000 in September, up 5.6% from a year earlier, even as sales slipped 2.0%. So this isn't a market where nothing sells. It's a market where buyers are picky, and the homes that miss on price or presentation get passed over.

San Clemente: the 12-day homes and the 84-day homes

This is the part I want every San Clemente seller to see. CRMLS data shows single-family homes that closed in September went under contract in a median of just 12 days. The single-family median sale price was $2.225 million, up from about $2.085 million a year earlier, and there were 101 active listings, down from 130.

Put those side by side. The homes that sold went fast. Meanwhile, roughly 43% of active listings had already reduced their price as of October 1, and expected market time was 84 days (down from 109 a year earlier). That's two different markets living on the same streets: homes priced and presented right on day one, and homes chasing the market down one price cut at a time.

Most expired listings I look at fall into that second group. It's rarely the house. It's usually the starting price, the photos or the marketing.

Dana Point and Capistrano Beach: same story, different numbers

Redfin's latest Dana Point data (through September) shows a median of 47 days on market and homes selling at about 98.3% of list price, with 26.7% of homes taking a price drop. Fewer cuts than San Clemente, but Dana Point and Capistrano Beach have a small number of sales each month, and many homes here sit above $2 million, where the county data says buyers have the most room to negotiate.

How much room? The Orange County Housing Report shows homes above $2.5 million closed an average of 3.6% below asking ($184,101 on average), better than 4.6% the week before. Homes under $2.5 million closed 1.9% below asking. On a $3 million Dana Point home, 3.6% is about $108,000 (my own math). That's why the starting price matters so much at the top end.

What I'd change before relisting

If your listing expired or you pulled it, here's how I'd approach the second try:

  • Re-price from today's sales, not last spring's. I'd pull the homes that actually closed near you in the last 60 to 90 days, not the ones still sitting. Buyers compare you to what's selling.
  • Start right, not high. A home that comes back at the right price can get the "new listing" attention all over again. One that comes back at the old price usually gets the same result.
  • Redo the photos and the story. New photos, a fresh description and, where it helps, light staging or a few repairs buyers flagged the first time.
  • Pick your timing on purpose. January is a popular relaunch window. Redfin counted a record of nearly 45,000 delisted homes relisted nationwide in January 2026, and over a third of them, 36.1%, came back below their original price. Going early means less competition. Waiting means a fresh market, but more homes to compete with.
  • Consider leasing in the meantime. If you don't need to sell right now, leasing for a year can make sense. I covered that math in last week's post.

Rates fell for a third straight week

Freddie Mac's survey for November 5 put the average 30-year fixed at 7.18%, down from 7.24% the week before, and the 15-year fixed at 6.57%. A year ago the 30-year was 6.22%.

On a $2.225 million San Clemente home with 20% down, principal and interest on a $1.78 million loan comes to about $12,058 a month at 7.18%, roughly $72 less than last week. It's still about $1,133 a month more than at last year's 6.22%. (My own math, principal and interest only.)

San Clemente: Measure M trailing

San Clemente voters weighed in on Measure M, a proposed 1-cent sales tax increase (from 7.75% to 8.75%) that city staff estimated could raise up to $15 million a year, split between beach sand replenishment and wildfire prevention like the city's goat grazing program. As of 1:36 a.m. on Wednesday, November 4, Voice of OC reported nearly 64% of voters opposed. Final counts are still coming in.

Whatever the result, sand and wildfire prep will stay big topics for coastal and canyon-edge homeowners here. With winter on the way, the city still offers free sand, bags and shovels at San Gorgonio Park and Richard T. Steed Memorial Park.

Dana Point Harbor: the Wharf is closed for construction

The Dana Point Wharf closed to the public on November 1 so work can start on Phase 4 of the roughly $600 million Dana Point Harbor Revitalization. Dana Wharf Sportfishing and Catalina Express moved into temporary trailers by the harbor parking structure, and Dana Wharf expects to run from there for about 15 months. The boardwalk, Baby Beach and the fishing pier stay open, and the Turkey Trot is still on for November 26.

For homeowners nearby in Dana Point and Capistrano Beach, a finished harbor should be a real draw for future buyers. If you're selling near the harbor during construction, plan showings around it.

My take this week

  • Your home didn't sell? Don't take it personally, and don't relist it unchanged. Price, photos and marketing are usually the fix.
  • Above $2.5 million? Buyers are still negotiating about 3.6% off asking on average. The right starting price protects your bottom line.
  • Thinking January? Use the next eight weeks to prep, so you launch with the strongest version of your home.
